One Layer. Short Sleeves. Still Rich.

Version 3, for a man who overheats. Nothing on this page is long-sleeved, nothing goes over anything else, and nothing needs an undershirt. One expensive short-sleeve top, lightweight dark jeans, Zegna sneakers, the Rolex. That is the actual uniform of wealthy men in Singapore and it is exactly one layer. Polo or tee? A polo is not necessary. Casual-classy is a well-made crew tee, dark straight jeans, suede shoes and the watch. Keep one collared polo for a formal Seoul corporate room, where a collar reads "chose to be here" and a tee reads "tech guy"; tees everywhere else.
Are expensive tees a statement? Half. Nobody sees the label. What is seen from ten feet: fabric weight and drape, a collar that lies flat, sleeves ending mid-bicep, a deep colour. A $90 Sunspel classic gets 85 percent of that; the $295 silk-cotton is a visible step in drape and sheen; above that (Zegna $950, Cucinelli) it is a statement only to people who own the same tee and know the cut, which in Seoul and Singapore luxury circles is real and everywhere else is invisible. Buy for how it hangs, not the number.
Why one layer still reads as money. The tell is the fabric, the fit and the shoes, not the number of layers. A silk-cotton or Sea Island tee that hangs right, dark clean denim hemmed to the shoe, suede Zegna sneakers, a Rolex: that outfit costs more than a suit and everyone who matters can see it. A crisp collar (polo) is the only thing you need for a meeting; Singapore business casual is a polo, full stop.

The formula

Meetings
Knit or piqué polo (collar), lightweight dark jeans, Triple Stitch, watch.
Everything else
Silk-cotton tee, same jeans, same shoes. Evening: swap to the white one.

Shoes

If you get the Zegnas (dark grey suede): wear navy on top with dark-rinse jeans as the default, white for evenings; charcoal and black work but flatten it. Avoid brown tones and mid-blue. Recognition: the three crossed elastic straps are the most recognised quiet-luxury shoe signature there is, and Zegna is enormous in Korea and Singapore; finance, tech and luxury people clock them instantly, everyone else sees expensive suede slip-ons. Breathing: honest, moderate. Leather-lined suede on rubber: fine in air conditioning, warm on a 32°C walk, no-show socks required. Zegna's espadrille version (jute midsole, ~$1,100 at Mitchells) breathes better; the Loro Piana Summer Walk (unlined suede, built for heat) is the best-breathing shoe at this tier and the quieter signal.

Where the money actually moves the needle

Ranked by how much a stranger across a lobby would notice. Most of the visible improvement is under $300; only one expensive item is worth its price.

1. Fit and hem: $25. The single biggest change. Jeans hemmed so they touch the shoe with no stack, a polo that sits on the shoulder seam and stops at the hip. Any tailor, same day. This is 40 percent of "looks rich" and it costs nothing.
2. Dark, clean denim: $218. Citizens Tropical in Rinse does everything the $725 pair does visually. Jacob Cohën buys a softer hand and a patch that only other Jacob Cohën owners recognize. Skip it unless the Seoul peer signal matters to you.
3. Shoes: $1,290, and this is the one wild price I will defend. Everyone looks at shoes. Suede Zegna slip-ons under a tee and jeans are what turn the outfit from "casual" into "money". If that is too much, the honest step down is Magnanni suede (Spain, ~$350 on Amazon), not a $150 sneaker.
4. Sunglasses: ~$350. Outdoors in Singapore you are wearing them 80 percent of the time, which makes them the most-seen thing on your face after the watch. Persol 714 folding (the Steve McQueen frame) is the quiet-rich pair; sold by Luxottica on Amazon, so it is genuine there.
5. The top's fabric: $140 to $295. Sunspel Riviera mesh polo and silk-cotton tee are the sweet spot. Going from $295 to the $950 Zegna tee is a texture only a hand on your sleeve can detect; going from $140 Riviera to the $500 Cucinelli polo buys a collar that holds its shape and a tonal logo a few people clock. Real but small.
6. Grooming. Fresh haircut the week before, nails, no visible sweat. Free, and undoes everything above if skipped.

Net: the $3,871 list works, but if you want the maximum visible gain per dollar it is hem + Citizens + Sunspel + Persol + Triple Stitch = about $2,300, and the Cucinelli, Jacob Cohën and Zegna tee are optional.

Rules for the trip

Sweaty feet, in order of effect: 1. aluminium-chloride foot antiperspirant at night, 3 to 4 nights then twice a week (Carpe; Certain Dri or Drysol if severe) · 2. merino or Coolmax no-show socks, never cotton (Falke, 12.5 to 13.5) · 3. rotate shoes, never two days running · 4. cedar shoe trees overnight and cedar insoles in sockless shoes · 5. Zeasorb or Gold Bond powder mornings · 6. unlined shoes (Loro Piana Summer Walk, Zegna espadrille) · 7. if none of it works it is hyperhidrosis and a dermatologist has iontophoresis or Botox. All four cheap items are in your Amazon cart: Carpe 2-pack $36.95, Falke no-shows $19, Zederna cedar insoles US 13 $36.90, Houndsbay cedar shoe trees XL 2-pack $49.99.
No undershirt. You said no layers; the tops above are dark or white and none of them show sweat the way a pale blue shirt does. If you change your mind, one Uniqlo AIRism under a polo is invisible and cooler than skin against piqué.
Seoul. A collared polo for every meeting; the Cucinelli or the Riviera. A silk-cotton tee is fine for dinner. Nobody expects a foreign visitor in a jacket in September heat, and a Rolex over a $500 polo is read correctly there.
Singapore. Polo or tee, jeans, Triple Stitch, every day. Every building is 19°C inside; if that bothers you it bothers you for twenty minutes, then you are back outside at 32°C. Do not carry a jacket for it.
